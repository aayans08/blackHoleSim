# Research brief: a deterministic, physically grounded generator for the procedural universe

Tags: **[E]** means essential for the first playable version. **[N]** means nice to have later. "verify" means I am quoting from memory or from a secondary source and the number should be checked against the primary paper before it is fixed in code. Everything else was checked against the cited sources during this research.

---

## 0. Pipeline overview (generation order)

```
galaxy_seed
 └─ octree sector (level k, ix,iy,iz)  → number of stars per luminosity bin (Poisson draw from the density model)
     └─ star_id = hash(sector, idx)    → population → age → [Fe/H] → initial mass → evolved state → binary → orbit in galaxy
         └─ (only near the player) system_id → disk → planets → orbits → moons/rings → physical properties
             └─ planet_id → surface params → cube-sphere tiles (seeded per tile) → terrain, ocean, ice, biome, clouds
```

Rule **[E]**: each stage reads only its own seed and the outputs of earlier stages. That keeps generation lazy, order-independent and repeatable.

## 1. Galaxy population (Milky Way)

### 1.1 Fixed parameters [E]
- Distance from the Sun to the Galactic Centre: R0 = 8.277 kpc. Mass of Sgr A*: 4.297×10⁶ M☉ ([GRAVITY 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.07478)). [Reid 2019](https://openaccess.inaf.it/handle/20.500.12386/31729) gives 8.15 ± 0.15 kpc.
- Rotation speed at the Sun: v_c(R0) = 229.0 km/s. Slope: dv_c/dR = −1.7 km/s/kpc for R between 5 and 25 kpc ([Eilers 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09466)).
- Solar peculiar motion: (U,V,W) = (11.1, 12.24, 7.25) km/s (Schönrich, Binney & Dehnen 2010, arXiv:0912.3693, verify).
- Local escape speed: 528 (+24/−25) km/s ([Deason 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.02016)). Koppelman & Helmi (2021) get 497 ± 8 km/s.
- Total mass M200 ≈ 1×10¹² M☉. Galactic year at the Sun ≈ 2π·8.2 kpc / 229 km/s ≈ 220 Myr. Unit conversion: 1 km/s ≈ 1.023 pc/Myr.

### 1.2 Stellar number density: Jurić et al. 2008, bias-corrected fit [E]
([Jurić 2008](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0510520). Values were confirmed through a [secondary analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.4551). Check Table 10 of ApJ 673, 864.)

```
ρ_D(R,z;L,H) = exp(-(R-R0)/L) · exp(-|z+z0|/H)
ρ(R,z) = ρ_thin,⊙ [ ρ_D(L1,H1) + f·ρ_D(L2,H2) + f_h·((R0² )/(R²+(z/q)²))^(n_h/2) ]
thin:  L1 = 2.6 kpc, H1 = 300 pc
thick: L2 = 3.6 kpc, H2 = 900 pc, f = 0.12 (local thick/thin)
halo:  f_h = 0.0051, q = c/a = 0.64, n_h = 2.77 (ρ ∝ r^-2.8)
z0 (Sun above plane) ≈ 25 pc
```

- Local normalisation: about 0.1 stars/pc³ including brown dwarfs, and stellar mass density ≈ 0.04 M☉/pc³ ([McKee+ 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.05334), verify exact numbers).
- **Bulge/bar [E]:** boxy/peanut bar with half-length ≈ 5 kpc, angle 27–30° to the Sun–GC line, mass ≈ 1.5–2×10¹⁰ M☉, pattern speed ≈ 39–41 km/s/kpc. Model it as an exponential or Gaussian triaxial bar ([Bland-Hawthorn & Gerhard 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07702), verify numbers).
- **Nuclear star cluster [N]:** about 2.5×10⁷ M☉, r_eff ≈ 4 pc, around Sgr A*.
- **Spiral arms [E for looks]:** four-arm logarithmic spiral, R(φ) = R_ref·exp((φ−φ_ref)·tan ψ). Pitch angles ψ range from about 7° to 20° per arm (Perseus ≈ 10°, Sagittarius beyond tangent ≈ 7°) ([Reid 2019/2014](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1401.5377v3)). Use a Gaussian arm cross-section of 0.3–0.6 kpc. Density contrast is only about 10–20% for old stars, but O/B stars, HII regions and dust are concentrated almost entirely in the arms, so weight the arm term by stellar age (strong for <100 Myr, weak for >1 Gyr). Pattern speed ≈ 20–30 km/s/kpc (verify).
- **Dust [E for looks]:** exponential layer with h_z ≈ 100–150 pc that follows the arms. The same field drives both the dark lanes in the Milky Way band and stellar extinction (Drimmel & Spergel 2001, verify).
- **Warp/flare [N].**

### 1.3 Ages and metallicity by population [E]
| Population | Age distribution | [Fe/H] |
|---|---|---|
| Thin disk | roughly constant SFR over 0–10 Gyr, gently declining (τ ≈ 8 Gyr); inner disk older on average (inside-out growth) | gradient ≈ −0.06 dex/kpc (Cepheids −0.060; young red giants −0.058; flattens to ≈ −0.03 for 6–10 Gyr stars), value at R0 ≈ 0, scatter σ ≈ 0.15–0.2 dex ([Anders 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.04951)) |
| Thick disk | 9–12 Gyr | ≈ −0.5 ± 0.3, [α/Fe] ≈ +0.3 |
| Halo | 11–13 Gyr | ≈ −1.5 ± 0.5 (outer halo more metal-poor) |
| Bulge | mostly 8–12 Gyr, small young component | ≈ 0, broad (−1 to +0.5) |

Choose the population for each star by the ratio of the density terms at its position (thin : thick : halo : bar).

### 1.4 Kinematics: so stars actually move [E]
- **Potential:** use an analytic three-component potential so any orbit can be evaluated. Recommended: MWPotential2014 (Bovy 2015, arXiv:1412.3451):
  - Miyamoto–Nagai disk: a = 3 kpc, b = 0.28 kpc, M ≈ 6.8×10¹⁰ M☉
  - NFW halo: r_s = 16 kpc
  - Power-law bulge with α ≈ 1.8 and cutoff 1.9 kpc
  
  Rescale it to v_c = 229 km/s and R0 = 8.28 kpc (it was fitted to 220 and 8). McMillan 2017 (arXiv:1608.00971) is the more modern alternative.
- **Disk stars use epicycle orbits.** This is analytic, O(1), and gives the position at any time t:
  ```
  Ω(R)=v_c/R,  κ² = R dΩ²/dR + 4Ω²  (flat curve → κ = √2 Ω ≈ 37 km/s/kpc at Sun),  ν² = ∂²Φ/∂z²
  R(t)=R_g + X cos(κt+φ_R)
  φ(t)=φ_g0 + Ω_g t − (2Ω_g/(κ R_g)) X sin(κt+φ_R)
  z(t)=Z cos(νt+φ_z)       (vertical period ≈ 60–90 Myr near the Sun)
  ```
  Get the amplitudes from velocity dispersions: X ≈ σ_R/κ and Z ≈ σ_z/ν, times a Rayleigh/Gaussian draw.
- **Age–velocity dispersion relation:** σ ∝ age^β, with β_R ≈ 0.25–0.3 and β_z ≈ 0.45–0.5 ([AVR fits](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.06556); [review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.04075)). Old thin disk at the Sun: (σ_R, σ_φ, σ_z) ≈ (40, 28, 22) km/s. Young OB stars: about 10 km/s. Scale with radius as σ ∝ exp(−R/(2L)).
- **Asymmetric drift:** a population with dispersion σ_R lags rotation by v_a ≈ σ_R²/80 km/s (Dehnen & Binney 1998).
- **Thick disk:** σ ≈ (60, 45, 40) km/s, lag ≈ 30–50 km/s.
- **Halo:** σ_r ≈ 140, σ_θ ≈ 75, σ_φ ≈ 85 km/s, almost no net rotation (Bond 2010, verify). Halo, bulge and other non-epicyclic stars need numerical orbit integration (leapfrog in the same potential) [N]. A cheaper option: integrate once at generation, store a coarse orbit table, and interpolate.

## 2. Stars

### 2.1 Initial mass function [E]
- **Kroupa 2001** (astro-ph/0009005), ξ(m) ∝ m^−α with:
  - α = 0.3 for 0.01–0.08 M☉ (brown dwarfs)
  - α = 1.3 for 0.08–0.5 M☉
  - α = 2.3 above 0.5 M☉
  
  Sample by piecewise inverse-CDF.
- **Chabrier 2003** (astro-ph/0304382) alternative:
  - For m < 1 M☉: ξ(log m) = 0.158·exp(−(log m − log 0.079)²/(2·0.69²))
  - For m > 1 M☉: ξ(log m) ∝ m^−1.3
- Upper limit is about 150 M☉. Brown dwarfs come out at roughly 1 per 4–5 stars.

### 2.2 Stellar evolution [E]
- **Use Hurley, Pols & Tout 2000 (SSE) analytic formulae** ([astro-ph/0001295](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0001295)). They give L, R, Teff, stage and remnant for 0.1–100 M☉ at any metallicity Z, cover ZAMS through remnant phases, and are within about 5% of detailed models. They were built for exactly this kind of fast population synthesis. Port the equations, not code.
- **Fallback if SSE comes later:**
  - Main-sequence luminosity:
    - L ≈ 0.23 M^2.3 for M < 0.43
    - L ≈ M^4 for 0.43–2
    - L ≈ 1.4 M^3.5 for 2–55
    - L ≈ 32000 M above 55
  - Radius: R ≈ M^0.8 for M < 1, R ≈ M^0.57 for M > 1.
  - Teff = 5772 K·(L/R²)^¼.
  - Main-sequence lifetime: t_MS ≈ 10 Gyr·M^−2.5.
  - Post-MS phases (subgiant, red giant branch, red clump/horizontal branch, AGB) last about 10% of t_MS.
  
  (verify; this set is textbook/Duric 2004.)
- **Spectral class by Teff:** O ≥ 30 kK, B 10–30 kK, A 7.5–10 kK, F 6–7.5 kK, G 5.2–6 kK, K 3.7–5.2 kK, M 2.4–3.7 kK, then L/T/Y brown dwarfs. Luminosity class comes from log g. Star colour should come from a Planck spectrum folded through the CIE matching functions, not a lookup table.
- **Remnants:**
  - **White dwarf** if M_i < ~8 M☉. Initial–final mass relation from Cummings 2018 MIST-based piecewise fit (verify the coefficients in [1809.01673](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01673)):
    - M_f = 0.080 M_i + 0.489 for 0.87–2.8 M☉
    - M_f = 0.187 M_i + 0.184 for 2.8–3.65 M☉
    - M_f = 0.107 M_i + 0.471 for 3.65–8.2 M☉
    
    White dwarf radius ≈ 0.0126 R☉·(M/0.6)^−⅓. Cooling follows Mestel, L ∝ t^−7/5; a 0.6 M☉ white dwarf is around 10⁻³ L☉ after roughly 1–2 Gyr.
  - **Neutron star or black hole** if M_i > 8 M☉. Explodability is not monotonic in mass ("islands"). Mean neutron star mass is ≈ 1.4 M☉. Mean black hole mass is ≈ 9 M☉ if only the helium core collapses and ≈ 14 M☉ if the whole star does ([Sukhbold 2016](https://www.doi.org/10.3847/0004-637X/821/1/38)). Practical rule: P(BH) rises from about 0 at 18 M☉ to about 0.8 at 30 M☉, with a few seeded islands. Neutron star R ≈ 12 km.
  - Galaxy totals for sanity checks: about 10⁸ stellar black holes and about 10⁹ neutron stars.

### 2.3 Binaries [E]
- **Multiplicity rises with primary mass:**
  - M dwarfs ≈ 27%
  - FGK ≈ 44–46% ([Raghavan 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.0414))
  - A/B > 50%
  - O ≥ 70%
- **Solar-type periods:** log-normal in log P (days), mean 5.03, σ 2.28 (Raghavan 2010, verify).
- **Joint P, q, e distribution [N]:** fit from [Moe & Di Stefano 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05347). Companion frequency peaks at log P ≈ 3.5 (≈ 10 AU). Short periods favour small q ≈ 0.2–0.3; long periods have thermal eccentricities.
- **Simple rules:** q roughly flat, with an excess of twins at q > 0.95. Orbits are circular below P ≈ 10 d; otherwise e is roughly uniform, or thermal for wide pairs.
- Binary orbits are Keplerian, so they are analytic.

### 2.4 Star clusters
- **Open clusters [E]:**
  - Cluster mass function dN/dM ∝ M^−2 for 10²–10⁵ M☉ ([Lada & Lada 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0301540)).
  - About half dissolve within about 10 Myr.
  - Disruption time t_dis = 1.3 ± 0.5 Gyr·(M/10⁴ M☉)^0.62 near the Sun ([Lamers 2005](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0505558)).
  - Generate a cluster only if age < t_dis. Members share age and [Fe/H] (scatter < 0.05 dex) and bulk velocity; internal σ is about 0.5–1 km/s.
  - King profile with r_c ≈ 1 pc and r_t ≈ 10–20 pc.
  - Young O/B associations sit in the spiral arms.
- **Globular clusters:** in the Milky Way, use the real Harris catalogue (about 157 objects). Procedural globulars are only for other galaxies [N]. Properties: 10–13 Gyr, bimodal [Fe/H] (≈ −1.5 / −0.5), r_h ≈ 3 pc, luminosity function peaking at M_V ≈ −7.4 with σ ≈ 1.2.

## 3. Special objects (the behaviour has to be right)

### 3.1 Hypervelocity stars (Hills mechanism) [E: the owner asked for this]
- **Ejection speed** ([Bromley 2006, eq. 1–4](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0608159)):
  ```
  v_ej = 1760 km/s · (a_bin/0.1 AU)^-1/2 · ((m1+m2)/2M☉)^1/3 · (M_BH/3.5e6 M☉)^1/6 · f_R
  f_R = 0.774 + (0.0204 + (−6.23e-4 + (7.62e-6 + (−4.24e-8 + 8.62e-11 D) D) D) D) D
  D   = (R_min/a_bin) · [2 M_BH / (1e6 (m1+m2))]^-1/3 ;   P_ej ≈ 1 − D/175 (0 for D>175)
  ```
  The other star is captured onto a tight orbit around Sgr A*, which is how S-stars form.
- **Galactic rate:** about 10⁻⁴ per year (Yu & Tremaine 2003; [Brown 2015 review](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2015ARA&A..53...15B)).
- **Behaviour:**
  - Isotropic directions from the Galactic Centre. Trajectories are nearly radial. Stars slow down as they climb out of the potential, so integrate them in the §1.4 potential.
  - A star is unbound if its speed at radius r exceeds v_esc(r), which is ≈ 530 km/s at the Sun.
  - Reference object: S5-HVS1, a 2.35 M☉ A-type star ejected at about 1800 km/s 4.8 Myr ago, now moving at 1755 ± 50 km/s about 9 kpc from the Sun ([Koposov 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.11725)).
- **Implementation:** a separate global high-velocity population, roughly 10⁴ objects (rate × lifetime), stored at the coarsest octree level and integrated once.

### 3.2 Runaway stars [E]
- Peculiar velocity above 30–40 km/s.
- Two production mechanisms:
  - Binary supernova kick (Blaauw 1961): the companion is released at about its orbital speed, typically 30–100 km/s and up to a few hundred.
  - Dynamical ejection from dense young clusters (Poveda 1967).
- Roughly 10–25% of O stars and a few % of B stars are runaways.
- Give them an origin cluster or OB association and trace them back along their velocity vector.

### 3.3 Neutron stars, pulsars, magnetars
- **Birth distributions** ([Faucher-Giguère & Kaspi 2006](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0512585)) [E]:
  - P₀ is Gaussian, mean 300 ms, σ 150 ms.
  - log B is Gaussian, mean 12.65, σ 0.55.
  - Kick: exponential 1-D components with scale 180 km/s (3-D mean ≈ 380 km/s). Hobbs 2005 gives a Maxwellian with σ = 265 km/s, but recent work says it overestimates the kicks ([Disberg & Mandel 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22102)).
  - Birth scale height ≈ 50 pc.
- **Spin-down by magnetic dipole** [E]:
  - B ≈ 3.2×10¹⁹·√(P·Ṗ) G
  - P(t) = √(P₀² + 2bt) with b = PṖ held constant
  - Characteristic age τ = P/(2Ṗ)
  - Radio emission stops past the death line B/P² ≲ 0.17×10¹² G s⁻² (Chen & Ruderman 1993, verify), after about 10–100 Myr.
  - Only about 10–20% of beams sweep past a given observer [N].
- **Magnetars [N]:** B ≈ 10¹⁴–10¹⁵ G, P ≈ 2–12 s, active for about 10⁴ yr.
- **Millisecond pulsars [N]:** spun up in binaries, P ≈ 1–10 ms, B ≈ 10⁸–10⁹ G.

### 3.4 Black holes and disks [E]
- **Disks only exist where there is fuel.** Isolated stellar black holes (the vast majority) are dark and act purely as lenses. Disks appear in X-ray binaries (Roche-lobe or wind accretion from the companion) and around SMBHs.
- **Thin disk** (Shakura & Sunyaev 1973):
  - T(r) = [3GMṀ/(8πσr³)·(1 − √(r_in/r))]^¼, peaking near r ≈ 1.36 r_in.
  - r_in = ISCO: 6GM/c² for Schwarzschild, 1–9 GM/c² for Kerr depending on spin (Bardeen 1972).
  - L = ηṀc², with η ≈ 0.057 (non-spinning) to 0.42 (maximal Kerr).
  - L_Edd = 1.26×10³¹ W·(M/M☉).
  - Peak temperature ≈ 10⁷ K for stellar black holes (X-rays) and ≈ 10⁵ K for SMBHs.
- **Sgr A* is not a thin disk.** It is a hot, puffed-up, radiatively inefficient flow at about 10⁻⁹–10⁻⁸ L_Edd (verify), and looks like the EHT ring. A bright film-style disk would be physically wrong for Sgr A*. It is fine for a quasar-like or procedural SMBH.
- **Hawking radiation:**
  - T_H = ħc³/(8πGMk_B) ≈ 6.2×10⁻⁸ K·(M☉/M)
  - Evaporation time t ≈ 5120πG²M³/(ħc⁴) ≈ 2×10⁶⁷ yr·(M/M☉)³
  - Every astrophysical black hole is colder than the CMB (2.725 K), so it absorbs more than it emits and Hawking radiation is invisible. Show it as a computed readout, not a glow. Only primordial black holes of ≲10¹¹–10¹² kg would be evaporating now, and those are hypothetical [N].

### 3.5 Rogue planets [E]
- Microlensing gives about 21 (+23/−13) free-floating or very-wide-orbit planets per star for 0.33–6660 M⊕, totalling about 80 M⊕ per star. The mass function is a power law, dN/dlogM ∝ M^−0.96, below about 0.02 M☉ ([Sumi 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08280)). So most rogues are Earth-mass or smaller.
- They are ejected at a few km/s, so they inherit the kinematics of their parent population.
- Rogue gas giants glow faintly in the infrared from internal heat (cooling tracks, Baraffe 2003, verify) [N].
- Generate rogues at the finest octree level only, since they are effectively invisible.

### 3.6 Nebulae
- **HII regions [E]:** around O/B associations in the arms, lifetimes ≲ 10 Myr. Strömgren radius R_s = (3Q/(4πn²α_B))^⅓ with α_B ≈ 2.6×10⁻¹³ cm³/s.
- **Dark nebulae / giant molecular clouds [E]:** from the dust field. GMC mass function dN/dM ∝ M^−1.6…−1.8 (verify).
- **Planetary nebulae [N]:** for white dwarf progenitors in the last ~2×10⁴ yr.
- **Supernova remnants [N]:** Galactic rate ≈ 1.6–2 per century. Sedov–Taylor radius R = 1.15·(E t²/ρ)^⅕.
- Inside the catalogued volume, prefer real catalogues.

## 4. Planetary systems

### 4.1 Formation-flavoured setup [E]
- **Disk:**
  - M_disk ≈ 0.01 M★, with log-normal scatter of about 0.5 dex.
  - Solid mass ∝ 10^[Fe/H].
  - Minimum-mass solar nebula template: Σ_gas = 1700·(r/AU)^−1.5 g/cm². Σ_solid = 7.1·(r/AU)^−1.5 inside the snow line and about 30·(r/AU)^−1.5 outside (Hayashi 1981).
- **Temperature and snow line:**
  - T(r) ≈ 280 K·(r/AU)^−½·(L/L☉)^¼.
  - Snow line at about 2.7 AU·(L/L☉)^½. Ida & Lin use 2.7 AU·(M★/M☉)² ([Ida & Lin 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0312144)).
- **Isolation mass** (b ≈ 10 Hill radii; derived from the feeding-zone width): M_iso = (2π b a² Σ_s)^{3/2} / (3M★)^{1/2}.
- **Core accretion:** cores above ~10 M⊕ that form before the gas disk disperses (lifetime about 3–5 Myr) undergo runaway gas accretion and become giants. Giants are therefore preferred at or beyond the snow line. Hot Jupiters come from migration.

### 4.2 Empirical occurrence: drives the statistics [E]
- **SAG13 baseline** ([Kopparapu 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.09602)), with R in R⊕ and P in years:
  - d²N/(dlnR dlnP) = Γ R^α P^β
  - R < 3.4: Γ = 0.38, α = −0.19, β = 0.26
  - R ≥ 3.4: Γ = 0.73, α = −1.18, β = 0.59
  - η⊕ ≈ 0.38 for FGK stars
- **Giant planets** (Cumming 2008, arXiv:0803.3357):
  - dN = C·M^−0.31·P^0.26·dlnM dlnP
  - 10.5% of FGK stars have a giant of 0.3–10 M_J with P = 2–2000 d
- **Metallicity and stellar-mass dependence of giants:**
  - f ≈ 0.03·10^(2.0[Fe/H]) (Fischer & Valenti 2005)
  - f ≈ 0.07·(M★/M☉)·10^(1.2[Fe/H]) (Johnson 2010, arXiv:1005.3084)
  - Small planets depend only weakly on metallicity.
- **M dwarfs:** about 2.5 planets per star with 1–4 R⊕ and P < 200 d (Dressing & Charbonneau 2015, arXiv:1501.01623). Giants are rare.
- **Hot Jupiters:** about 0.5–1% of FGK stars.
- **Radius valley:** at 1.5–2.0 R⊕ (Fulton 2017, arXiv:1703.10375). Have it emerge from photoevaporation (Owen & Wu 2017, arXiv:1705.10810): strip the H/He envelope of low-mass cores at high early XUV insolation.
- **Size and insolation classes** (Kopparapu 2018), radius in R⊕:
  - rocky 0.5–1.0
  - super-Earth 1.0–1.75
  - sub-Neptune 1.75–3.5
  - sub-Jovian 3.5–6
  - Jovian 6–14.3

### 4.3 Mass–radius and composition [E]
- **Mass–radius** (Chen & Kipping 2017, [1603.08614](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.08614v1.pdf)):
  - R ∝ M^0.28 below about 2 M⊕
  - R ∝ M^0.59 from 2 to about 130 M⊕
  - R ∝ M^−0.04 from 130 M⊕ to about 0.08 M☉
  - Include the intrinsic scatter.
- **Rocky planets:** R/R⊕ = (1.07 − 0.21·CMF)·(M/M⊕)^(1/3.7), valid for 1–8 M⊕ and core mass fraction 0–0.4 ([Zeng 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.08827)). Earth CMF ≈ 0.33. Tie CMF to the host star's Fe/Mg [N].
- **Water:** about 0.01–0.1 wt% for bodies formed inside the snow line, up to about 50% for bodies formed beyond it (water worlds).
- **Sub-Neptune envelopes:** 1–5% H/He by mass (Lopez & Fortney 2014, arXiv:1311.0329) [N].
- **Hot Jupiter inflation:** radius grows with irradiation above T_eq ≈ 1000 K [N].

### 4.4 Architecture and stability [E]
- **Spacing:**
  - Mutual Hill radius: R_H,m = [(m1+m2)/(3M★)]^⅓·(a1+a2)/2, with Δ = (a2−a1)/R_H,m.
  - Two planets are Hill-stable if Δ > 2√3 ≈ 3.46 (Gladman 1993).
  - For multi-planet systems stable over Gyr, require Δ ≳ 8–10 (Chambers 1996; Pu & Wu 2015).
  - Sample Δ log-normal with median ≈ 20 (Kepler multis).
  - "Peas in a pod": neighbouring planets have similar sizes and regular spacing (Weiss 2018, arXiv:1706.06204).
- **Resonances [N]:** period ratios pile up just wide of 3:2 and 2:1 (Fabrycky 2014, arXiv:1202.6328). Put about 10–15% of adjacent pairs near mean-motion resonances, and add occasional resonant chains in young systems.
- **Eccentricities:**
  - Giant/RV planets: Beta(0.867, 3.03) (Kipping 2013, arXiv:1306.4982).
  - Compact multis: Rayleigh with σ ≈ 0.02–0.05.
- **Inclinations:** mutual inclinations Rayleigh with σ ≈ 1–2°.
- **Binary hosts:** truncate planets with the Holman & Wiegert 1999 critical radius (astro-ph/9809315, verify coefficients). S-type: a_c/a_b = 0.464 − 0.380μ − 0.631e + 0.586μe + 0.150e² − 0.198μe².
- **Belts [N]:** asteroid belt inside the innermost giant's orbit, Kuiper belt beyond the outermost giant.
- **Orbital motion:** planet and moon orbits are Keplerian, solved with Kepler's equation by Newton iteration in f64, so they are analytic at any time. Use N-body only for showcase scenes [N].

### 4.5 Moons and rings
- **Moons [E]:**
  - Stable prograde moons orbit within about 0.49 r_H; retrograde within about 0.93 r_H (Domingos 2006, MNRAS 373, 1227).
  - Hill radius r_H = a(m/3M★)^⅓.
  - Regular satellites of giants total about 10⁻⁴ of the planet's mass (Canup & Ward 2006, verify) and sit in a few near-resonant, nearly circular orbits.
  - Terrestrial planets get large moons from rare giant impacts.
  - Irregular moons are distant, eccentric, and often retrograde.
- **Rings [N]:**
  - They sit inside the fluid Roche limit, d = 2.44·R_p·(ρ_p/ρ_m)^⅓.
  - Icy rings beyond the snow line, rocky or dusty rings inside it.
  - Bright massive rings are rare; faint dusty rings are common.

## 5. Planet physical properties

### 5.1 Equilibrium temperature [E]
- T_eq = T★·(R★/2a)^½·[f(1−A)]^¼, which is about 279 K·(1−A)^¼·(L/L☉)^¼·(a/AU)^−½.
- f = 1 for full heat redistribution, up to 8/3 for a tidally locked dayside.
- Bond albedos: Earth 0.30, Venus 0.76, Mars 0.25, Moon 0.11, ices 0.5–0.9.

### 5.2 Greenhouse effect [E]
- Grey-atmosphere model: T_s = T_eq·(1 + ¾τ)^¼. Earth τ ≈ 0.84 (288 vs 255 K). Venus τ ≈ 140 (737 vs 229 K).
- τ scales roughly with surface pressure times greenhouse-gas fraction.
- **Habitable zone** (Kopparapu 2013/2014, arXiv:1301.6674, 1404.5292): S_eff = S☉ + aT + bT² + cT³ + dT⁴ with T = Teff − 5780 K ([coefficients](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4156685)):
  - Runaway greenhouse (inner edge): S☉ = 1.107, a = 1.332e-4, b = 1.58e-8, c = −8.308e-12, d = −1.931e-15
  - Maximum greenhouse (outer edge): S☉ = 0.356, a = 6.171e-5, b = 1.698e-9, c = −3.198e-12, d = −5.575e-16
  - Valid for Teff = 2600–7200 K.
- Wet planets inside the runaway limit become Venus-like (thick CO₂).

### 5.3 Atmosphere retention [E]
- **Jeans escape:** escape parameter λ = GMm/(kT_exo·r). A gas is retained over Gyr if v_esc ≳ 6× its mean thermal speed at the exobase (λ ≳ 40). Note that T_exo ≫ T_eq (Earth ≈ 1000 K).
- **Cosmic shoreline:** bodies keep atmospheres if insolation I ≲ k·v_esc⁴ ([Zahnle & Catling 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.03386)).
  - My own calibration from Solar System bodies (marginal Mars and Callisto; check against their Fig. 1): I/I⊕ ≈ (v_esc / 6 km/s)⁴.
  - A 3-D refit finds a steeper exponent, ≈ 5.9 (Berta-Thompson, arXiv:2507.02136).
  - M-dwarf planets: shift the line toward atmosphere loss because of high XUV flux.

### 5.4 Atmosphere composition classes [E]
- Gas giants: H₂/He.
- Sub-Neptunes: H/He with haze.
- Terrestrials, by volatiles and temperature:
  - Airless (Mercury-like)
  - Thick CO₂ (Venus-like)
  - N₂/H₂O (Earth-like), plus O₂ if life is present
  - Thin CO₂ (Mars-like)
  - N₂/CH₄ with tholin haze (Titan-like) when T ≲ 100 K and the body can hold gas
- Scale height H = kT/(μ m_u g); Earth's is 8.5 km. This sets the rim-glow thickness. Rayleigh scattering (∝ λ⁻⁴) sets the sky colour.

### 5.5 Tidal locking, rotation, tilt
- **Despin time [E]:** t_lock ≈ ω a⁶ I Q / (3 G m★² k₂ R⁵) (Gladman 1996). Habitable-zone planets of M dwarfs (≲ 0.5 M☉) and almost all moons are locked.
- **Spin–orbit resonances [N]:** 3:2 for eccentric orbits (Mercury). A thick atmosphere can prevent locking (Venus).
- **Primordial rotation [E]:** giants spin at about 0.1–0.3 of breakup (about 9–17 h). Terrestrial rotation is random from giant impacts (log-normal around about 10–30 h).
- **Obliquity [E]:** giants mostly below 30°, with rare outliers like Uranus. Terrestrials have a broad distribution. Without a large moon, obliquity is chaotic (Laskar 1993) [N].
- **Seasons [E]:** daily-mean insolation by latitude is Q = (S/π)·(a/r)²·(h₀ sinφ sinδ + cosφ cosδ sin h₀), with cos h₀ = −tanφ tanδ.

### 5.6 Habitability flag [E]
A planet is flagged habitable if all of these hold:
- it is inside the Kopparapu habitable zone,
- its mass is 0.1–5 M⊕,
- it keeps an atmosphere,
- liquid water is stable,
- the star is older than about 1 Gyr.

Life is a probabilistic, seeded flag on top of that, which drives O₂ and vegetation. Pigment colour by spectral type is speculative [N].

## 6. Surface appearance driven by physics

### 6.1 Craters [E]
- **Density from age:** Neukum 2001 chronology, N(≥1 km)/km² = 5.44×10⁻¹⁴·(e^{6.93T} − 1) + 8.38×10⁻⁴·T, with T in Gyr ([quoted in](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.03292)).
- **Size distribution:** cumulative N(≥D) ∝ D^−2 (approximation to the Neukum production function). Cap at equilibrium, about 10% of geometric saturation, N_gs = 1.54·D⁻² (Gault 1970, verify).
- **Scaling to other bodies:**
  - Scale the impact flux per body.
  - Use the resurfacing age, not the formation age.
  - An atmosphere screens out small impactors; erosion removes old craters.
- **Morphology:**
  - Simple-to-complex transition D_sc ∝ 1/g: Moon ≈ 15–20 km, Mars ≈ 7 km, Earth ≈ 3 km.
  - Simple craters: depth/D ≈ 0.2, rim height ≈ 0.04 D. Ejecta thickness ∝ (r/R)⁻³.
  - Complex craters have central peaks; above about 4–5·D_sc they become peak rings, then multi-ring basins.
  - Bright rays fade after about 1 Gyr.

### 6.2 Tectonics, volcanism, relief [E]
- **Internal heat:** radiogenic H(t) = Σ H_i e^{−λ_i t}, plus tidal heating ∝ (k₂/Q)·n⁵·e²·R⁵/G (the Io case). Cooling time grows as R², so small bodies die early.
- **Regime choice:** plate tectonics needs liquid water and roughly Earth-like mass. The mass dependence is debated (Valencia 2007 vs O'Neill & Lenardic 2007), so treat it as probabilistic. Otherwise the planet is stagnant-lid (Mars/Venus-like).
- **Hypsometry:** plate tectonics gives a bimodal height distribution (continents and ocean basins), with Voronoi plates (about 7–15 for an Earth-size planet) and mountain belts at the boundaries. Stagnant lid gives a unimodal distribution with large shield volcanoes.
- **Maximum relief** scales as 1/g: Earth about 9–10 km, Mars about 22 km.
- **Surface colour from chemistry:**
  - Basalt: dark grey
  - Oxidised iron: red; needs a water or oxidant history
  - Sulfur: yellow; requires tidal volcanism
  - Ices: white or bluish
  - Tholins: red-brown; cold, CH₄, and UV
  - Lava worlds: dayside magma ocean at T ≳ 1500 K
  - Anything above about 800 K glows visibly on its night side (Draper point), including lava worlds and hot Jupiters.

### 6.3 Water, ice, erosion, biomes
- **Sea level [E]:** fill the hypsometric curve until it holds the water inventory. Water is liquid where 273 K < T < T_boil(P_s) (Clausius–Clapeyron).
- **Ice caps [E]:** where annual-mean T < ~263 K.
- **Latitudinal temperature [N, E-lite]:** use the Budyko–Sellers/North energy-balance model,
  C ∂T/∂t = Q(1−α(T)) − (A + BT) + ∂ₓ[D(1−x²)∂ₓT],
  with Earth values A ≈ 203 W/m², B ≈ 2.1 W/m²/K, D ≈ 0.55. The ice–albedo feedback lets snowball worlds emerge on their own.
- **Tidally locked "eyeball" worlds [N]:** ocean only at the substellar point.
- **Erosion [N, high visual value]:**
  - Hydraulic erosion by the stream-power law, E = K A^m S^n with m/n ≈ 0.5, run on the CPU over tile heightmaps.
  - Thermal erosion with a talus angle of 30–35°.
  - Erosion strength scales with precipitation, which needs liquid water plus an atmosphere.
  - Aeolian features (dunes) on dry worlds with atmospheres.
- **Biomes [E for habitable worlds]:** Whittaker temperature–precipitation diagram, or Köppen classes. Precipitation comes from distance to ocean and Hadley-cell bands: deserts near the cell edge (about 30° on Earth), and the number of cells grows with rotation rate.

### 6.4 Gas giants [E]
- **Sudarsky classes** ([Sudarsky 2000](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9910504)):

  | Class | Teff | Clouds | Appearance |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | I | ≲ 150 K | NH₃ | Jupiter-like tan/brown bands |
  | II | ~150–250 K | H₂O | white, highest albedo |
  | III | ~350–800 K | none | azure; Rayleigh scattering plus Na/K absorption |
  | IV | 900–1500 K | silicate deck deep | darkest |
  | V | ≳ 1500 K | high silicate/iron | brighter again |

- **Ice giants:** CH₄ absorbs red light, so they look cyan or blue.
- **Band count:** jet width follows the Rhines scale, L_β = π√(2U/β) with β = 2Ω cosφ/R, so the number of jets N ∝ (ΩR/U)^½. Calibrate the constant so Jupiter has about 20 jets and Uranus/Neptune about 4 ([Showman review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0911.3170); [Gastine 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.3679)).
- **Hot Jupiters:** tidally locked, so few broad bands, a superrotating equatorial jet, and the hot spot shifted east.
- **Vortices and polar hexagons [N].**

### 6.5 Clouds on rocky worlds [E]
- Earth-like: H₂O clouds, coverage about 0.6. Pattern follows the circulation: ITCZ, mid-latitude cyclones, cell count set by rotation.
- Venus: H₂SO₄ deck.
- Mars: CO₂ and H₂O ice clouds plus dust.
- Titan: organic haze.
- Slowly rotating or tidally locked planets: thick substellar cloud deck [N].

## 7. Determinism, laziness and streaming [E]

1. **Keys and hashing**
   - Keys:
     - seed(level, ix, iy, iz) = H(galaxy_seed, level, ix, iy, iz)
     - object = H(sector_seed, index)
     - property stream = H(object_seed, PROPERTY_TAG)
   - Use a stateless 64-bit integer hash (SplitMix64 or PCG finalizer, wyhash or xxh3), plus small per-object PRNGs seeded from it.
   - Per-property tags mean that adding a new property later never shifts existing ones.
2. **Sparse octree with luminosity-stratified levels.**
   - Level 0 is a cube of about 32 kpc; each level halves the size, so level 15 is about 1 pc.
   - Each luminosity bin b lives at exactly one level k(b), chosen so the cell size ≈ d_max(b)/2, where d_max = 10 pc·10^((m_lim − M_b)/5).
   - O/B supergiants live in kpc cells; M dwarfs, white dwarfs, brown dwarfs and rogue planets only in pc cells.
   - The camera loads a fixed neighbourhood of about 5³ cells at every level, so the cost is O(levels) and independent of the scene.
   - Since every star belongs to exactly one level, there are no duplicates.
3. **Counts per cell.**
   - N ~ Poisson(λ), with λ = ∫ n(x)·φ_b dV.
   - n(x) is the §1.2 density and φ_b is the fraction of the present-day luminosity function in bin b.
   - Evaluate λ with a few-point quadrature over the cell. Use seeded Poisson sampling: inverse transform for small λ, PTRS or a normal approximation for large λ.
   - Place positions by seeded rejection against the local density gradient.
4. **Template tables for star properties.**
   - At startup, Monte Carlo the full chain (IMF × star formation history × SSE evolution) per population: about 10⁶ samples, well under a second.
   - Bucket the results by luminosity bin and store them in RAM only.
   - A star picks a template by hashed index, then jitters its continuous parameters and applies the local [Fe/H] offset.
   - This makes each bin exactly consistent with the joint distribution, with nothing written to disk.
5. **Time.**
   - Store orbit constants (epicycle parameters and phases), not positions, so position(t) is analytic.
   - Option A: define sectors in a frame co-rotating with each guiding centre, (R_g, φ_g − Ω(R_g)·t, z), so cell membership never changes over time.
   - Option B: assign membership at epoch t₀ and pad queries by v_max·|t − t₀|.
   - If time can be fast-forwarded by Myr, re-evaluate stellar stage with SSE at the new age. Bin stars by the maximum luminosity they reach within the allowed time window, so they never need to change level.
   - Hypervelocity stars and halo objects go in a small global list at level 0.
6. **Consistency between resolved stars and glow.** The same density, luminosity-function and dust fields drive the volumetric Milky Way band and unresolved glow. The band and the individual stars therefore agree at every distance.
7. **Merging real catalogues.** Inside the catalogue completeness distance for each luminosity bin, suppress procedural stars and insert the real stars into the same level structure. Use real Milky Way objects (Solar System, Gaia/HYG stars, Harris globulars, known black holes) where they exist; procedural generation only fills in beyond them.
8. **Planets and surfaces on demand.**
   - system_id = H(star_id); tile seed = H(planet_id, face, lod, x, y).
   - Planets use cube-sphere quadtrees with per-tile local origins.
   - Positions: i64 sector index plus f64 local offset, rendered relative to the camera. A galaxy-scale float can't hold metre precision.
9. **CPU and GPU roles.**
   - The CPU is authoritative for everything discrete: whether an object exists, counts, stage, orbits. Use integer math or the pure-Rust `libm` crate for transcendental functions, so results don't depend on the platform's floating-point library.
   - The GPU only adds visual detail: noise octaves, clouds, shading. GPU results must never feed back into generation.
   - CPU cells are generated in parallel on worker threads (e.g. rayon).
   - An LRU cache under a RAM budget; nothing generated is persisted to disk (3.4 GB limit).
10. **Tests.** Golden hashes of sector contents for fixed seeds, plus statistical tests checking the generated output against the distributions above:
    - local density ≈ 0.1/pc³
    - binary fraction by stellar mass
    - η⊕ ≈ 0.38
    - roughly 21 rogue planets per star
