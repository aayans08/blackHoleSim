# Rust renderer reconnaissance: `/Users/aayan/Documents/GitHub/blackHoleSim`

**Bottom line:** the code compiles, but the program cannot show a black hole. A uniform-layout mismatch sets the shader's Schwarzschild radius and field of view to 0, so every frame is one flat colour. Fixing that alone still shows nothing, because each ray only travels 15 km. The light-bending formula is a heuristic, not a geodesic, and most of the disk physics is wrong or decorative. Keep the wgpu/winit setup and the HDR-texture → fullscreen-triangle pattern. Rewrite the physics, the starfield, the camera/units and the post-processing.

## 3. Physics audit

### 3.0 Bugs that stop anything from rendering

**A. Uniform layout mismatch.** In WGSL, `vec3<f32>` takes 12 bytes but is aligned to 16, so a following `f32` packs into its 4th lane. Offsets printed by naga:

| Shader field | WGSL offset | Rust field at that offset | Value the shader reads |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Camera.fov` (compute.wgsl:9) | 60 | `up[3]` (main.rs:94) | **0.0** |
| `Camera.aspect` | 64 | `fov` | 1.309 |
| `BlackHole.schwarzschild_radius` (compute.wgsl:16) | 12 | `position[3]` (main.rs:235) | **0.0** |
| `BlackHole.mass` | 16 | `schwarzschild_radius` | 29.54 |

What follows from that:
- `tan(fov/2) = 0`, so every pixel's ray equals `camera.forward` (compute.wgsl:299-305).
- `rs = 0`, so there is no horizon, no deflection, `v_kepler = 0`, and the redshift is 1.
- **Every frame is one uniform colour.** I evaluated the shader by hand for that single ray: environment texel × 0.923 plus about 1e-4 of disk haze. On screen that is about RGB(86,117,151), a slate blue. Turning the camera only changes that one colour. I did not run the binary; this comes from the naga offsets plus hand evaluation of the shader, which is deterministic.
- `DiskParams` (all f32) matches the Rust struct.

**B. Step budget.** `dt = 0.1` km and 150 steps (compute.wgsl:216-217) give a 15 km maximum path.
- The camera is 301 km from the hole and the escape radius is 2000 km (218).
- Every ray therefore ends in the "max steps" branch (280-282). None reaches the horizon or escapes.
- Simulated with the shader's own integrator: impact parameters b = 1.5, 2.6 and 5 Rs all return `maxsteps`.
- Even with the layout fixed, there is **no shadow and no lensing** (only about a 0.4° uniform nudge), and the disk is sampled only within 15 km of the eye.

**C. The start view misses the hole.** Forward is (0, −0.199, −0.980). The hole is 87.8° off-axis, but the horizontal half field of view at aspect 1.6 is about 50.8°.

**D. Image flipped vertically.** `ndc.y = uv.y*2−1` with row 0 at the top (compute.wgsl:295-296), combined with the display UVs (display.wgsl:18-22), puts "down" at the top of the screen.

### 3.1 Light bending is a heuristic, not a null geodesic

The code (compute.wgsl:64-93): `a = (1.5·Rs/r²)·(r̂_in + v_⊥/r)`, then explicit Euler, then `normalize(v)`.

The correct form (Binet equation u″+u = (3/2)Rs·u² written in Cartesian coordinates; see "Starless", https://rantonels.github.io/starless/) is:

  d²x/dλ² = −(3/2)·Rs·h²·x / r⁵,  with h = |x × v| conserved.

Differences:

1. **Missing h²/r² factor.** The code's acceleration has magnitude 1.5·Rs/r² for every ray. The correct one is 1.5·Rs·sin²α/r², where α is the angle between the ray and the radial direction. After normalisation, the code's sideways bending ∝ sinα while the correct one ∝ sin²α. The two agree only at closest approach and photon-sphere radius (1.5 Rs).
2. **Measured error.** I integrated the shader's force with a tiny step and compared it with RK4 on the correct equation:

   | Quantity | Shader force law | Correct |
   |---|---|---|
   | Critical impact parameter (shadow radius) | **4.03 Rs** | 2.598 Rs = 3√3/2 Rs (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photon_sphere) |
   | Deflection at b = 5 Rs | 1.372 rad | 0.590 rad (×2.32) |
   | Deflection at b = 10 Rs | 0.400 rad | 0.236 rad (×1.69) |
   | Deflection at b = 50 Rs | 0.063 rad | 0.041 rad (×1.52) |

   Analytically, the code's weak-field deflection is 3Rs/b instead of GR's 2Rs/b = 4GM/(c²b) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravitational_lens). The shadow is 55% too large.
3. The `v_⊥/r` term adds a dimensionless quantity to one in 1/km, so the result depends on the unit system (compute.wgsl:83). It has no physical basis.
4. `normalize(v)` every step (compute.wgsl:91) is wrong for the correct form. There λ is not arc length and h must stay constant; renormalising v rescales h.
5. Explicit Euler (first order) with a fixed step (compute.wgsl:87-93). It needs RK4 or an adaptive step (Δλ ∝ r), or a precomputed deflection lookup table. For the lookup-table approach see Bruneton 2020, real-time Schwarzschild rendering: https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08735.
6. `geodesic_acceleration` assumes the hole is at the origin (`-pos/r`, compute.wgsl:74), while `trace_ray` measures r from `black_hole.position` (224). These disagree as soon as the hole moves.
7. Camera rays are built directly in coordinate space (compute.wgsl:301-305). Near the hole they need a static-observer frame transform (the radial component scaled by √(1−Rs/r)). There is no aberration either.
8. Dead code: `schwarzschild_metric` (53-61), `GeodesicState.energy/angular_momentum` (48-49), and the constant `G` (40) are never used.
9. CLAUDE.md's own "RK4/Christoffel" example has the same kind of heuristic error (`deflection_radial = -rs/(r²·f)`, `post_newtonian_correction`). Rewrite it when CLAUDE.md is updated.

### 3.2 Accretion disk

1. **Temperature profile** (117-131). The comment says T ∝ r^−0.75. The code instead does `mix(T_in, T_out, pow(x, 0.75))`, a linear blend in temperature.
   - Correct (Shakura–Sunyaev / Page–Thorne): T(r) = T*·(r/r_in)^−3/4·[1 − √(r_in/r)]^1/4. It is zero at the ISCO (innermost stable circular orbit) and peaks at about 1.36·r_in (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1974ApJ...191..499P).
   - The parameters are also inconsistent: from 3 Rs to 12 Rs, an r^−3/4 law drops by 2.8×, but the code drops by 1000× (1e7 → 1e4 K).
2. **Blackbody colour** (134-161). A hand-made piecewise palette keyed on T/10⁴, not Planck's law integrated against the CIE colour-matching functions (reference table: http://www.vendian.org/mncharity/dir3/blackbody/).
   - With T at or above 1e4 everywhere, almost the whole disk is the constant (0.7, 0.8, 1.0).
   - Cool temperatures come out over-saturated: 3000 K gives (1, 0.6, 0) where the true colour is about (1, 0.7, 0.42).
3. **Brightness** (157-158). `0.05·log10(T/1000)`, clamped to [0.001, 0.5]. Stefan–Boltzmann flux goes as T⁴, so the real brightness range is about 10¹² while the code's is about 3×. More scaling hacks: ×0.01 (256), a per-step cap of 0.1 (259), and an accumulation cap of 5 (269).
4. **Doppler sign reversed** (179). The code uses `1 + v·d̂/c`, where d̂ is the ray-march direction (away from the camera). The photon actually travels along −d̂, so material receding from the camera gets brightened. Correct: g = 1/(γ(1 − β·k̂)) with k̂ pointing toward the observer.
5. **Doppler is first order only.** No Lorentz factor γ, no transverse Doppler, and a clamp to [0.5, 2] (249).
6. **Orbital speed is Newtonian** (171): `c·√(Rs/2r)` gives 0.408c at the ISCO. A static observer in GR measures β = √(Rs/(2(r−Rs))), which is 0.5c at 3 Rs (Bardeen–Press–Teukolsky 1972, https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1972ApJ...178..347B).
7. **Gravitational redshift** (185-187). `√(1−Rs/r)` is correct only for a static emitter. Orbiting gas needs the combined factor:
   - Local form: g = √(1−Rs/r)·√(1−β²)/(1−β·cosψ). At cosψ = 0 this reduces to √(1−3Rs/2r).
   - Equivalent Luminet form: 1+z = (1+Ω·b_z)/√(1−3M/r) (https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1979A%26A....75..228L; DNGR: https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.03808).
   - The code also evaluates r before the step (224 vs 238).
8. **Beaming and colour shift missing.** g is applied linearly to intensity (250). It should be I_ν ∝ g³ (or g⁴ bolometric), and the observed temperature should be T_obs = g·T_emit, which shifts the colour. The colour is never shifted.
9. **Geometry and density.**
   - Scale height grows as H ∝ r, giving H/r ≈ 0.17 (107). That is a thick, Gaussian-tailed haze rather than a thin disk.
   - Density ∝ r^−2.75 (111) is not a standard profile; Shakura–Sunyaev gives ρ ∝ r^−33/20 to r^−15/8.
   - The inner edge is a hard cut with no zero-torque taper.
10. **Absorption uses an arbitrary scale.** κ = density × 0.3 per km (262) is not tied to any physical opacity.
11. **What is structurally correct:** the emission–absorption update `I += S·T·(1−e^{−κΔs})`, `T *= e^{−κΔs}` (266-271), i.e. a local-thermal-equilibrium source function.
12. **Missing entirely:**
    - Kerr (spinning) black holes.
    - Hawking radiation. For 10 M☉, T_H ≈ 6.2×10⁻⁹ K and power ≈ 9×10⁻³¹ W, so it is invisible except for primordial holes below about 10¹¹ kg (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawking_radiation).
    - Disk self-shadowing beyond Beer's law.
    - Time or orbital animation of the disk.

## 4. Rendering quality issues

1. **Double sRGB encoding.** An sRGB swapchain (main.rs:203-208) plus manual `linear_to_srgb` (display.wgsl:79) washes everything out. The background shows as RGB(86,117,151) where a single encode gives (24,45,79).
2. **Space renders blue-grey, not black.** The background (5,10,20) is stored in non-sRGB `Rgba8Unorm` (main.rs:529-535, 569), so those bytes are read as linear values.
3. **Starfield**
   - The LCG returns values in [0, 2) (main.rs:541), so only about 25% of the 50,000 stars land in the texture (about 12.5k).
   - Brightness `128 + r·127` saturates `as u8`, so about half the stars are pure 255.
   - All stars are white single texels with no magnitude or colour distribution and no Milky Way band.
   - They are uniform in (u,v), so density goes as 1/sinθ and stars crowd the poles.
   - A square 2048² texture for a 360°×180° map gives 2:1 non-square texels (0.176° × 0.088°), so stars are stretched horizontally.
   - Texel density is 5.7 texels/° against about 25 px/° on the 2560-px retina at roughly 102° horizontal FOV. Stars are magnified about 4× into bilinear blobs: the "pixelated" look.
   - No mipmaps (mip_level_count 1 at main.rs:566, `textureSampleLevel(...,0)` at compute.wgsl:200), so lensed regions alias and shimmer.
   - The sampler's v axis is Repeat (main.rs:284) where it should be ClampToEdge, so samples near the poles wrap to the opposite edge.
4. **No anti-aliasing.** One sample per pixel at the pixel centre (compute.wgsl:295) and no temporal accumulation, so the horizon edge and photon ring will alias.
5. **HDR range thrown away.** Caps at 0.01, 0.1 and 5 in compute.wgsl, another clamp to 5 in display.wgsl:69, and fixed exposure 0 (72). No auto-exposure, no bloom or glare point-spread function (which stars and the disk need), and per-channel ACES (hue shifts). No dithering, so 8-bit gradients band.
6. **Precision.** f32 kilometres. The spacing between representable values is 16 km at 1 AU and about 1.05×10⁶ km at 1 light-year. The universe scale needs f64 world coordinates on the CPU and camera-relative f32 on the GPU (floating origin), plus reversed-Z or logarithmic depth for any raster geometry.
7. **Performance.** Full 2560×1600 ray march every frame with a fixed 150-iteration loop and no adaptive stepping, even though CLAUDE.md claims adaptive stepping. Covering the scene properly at 0.1 km steps would take about 20,000 steps per pixel.
8. **Debug leftovers.** The magenta NaN output (display.wgsl:64-66) is still on. `SurfaceError::Outdated` and `Timeout` are not handled. There is no FPS counter or GPU timing.
9. **Gap to the reference screenshot.** Nothing exists for planets, atmospheric scattering (rim glow), cloud layers, terrain, point-sprite stars, clusters or the galactic band. The architecture is ray-trace-only with no raster path.
