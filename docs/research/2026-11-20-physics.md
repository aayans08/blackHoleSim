# Technical brief: physics and rendering research for the Rust + wgpu universe explorer (M3 Air / Metal)

I did not read or change anything in the repo. Units are G = c = M = 1 inside the black-hole equations unless a line says otherwise; rs = 2M. Reference screenshot reviewed. For "crisp" stars through a lens, splat each star as a point-spread function (PSF) and scale it by the lens magnification. Do not sample a texture (see 1.6).

## 0. Hard platform facts that drive the design

- **No 64-bit floats on the GPU.** Apple GPUs have no FP64 hardware and Metal does not expose it, so WGSL `f64` will not work on M3. All double-precision physics (ephemerides, n-body, ship state) runs on the CPU. The GPU gets camera-relative `f32`, and the black-hole shaders work in units of M (r ≈ 1–1000).
- **Coordinates.** For universe-scale positions, use Rust `i128` fixed point at 1 µm. That reaches ±1.7×10³² m, compared with 4.4×10²⁶ m for the observable universe. Subtract in i128, convert to f64 for local work, then to f32 for the GPU.
- **Time.** f64 seconds lose about 3 s of precision at 1 Gyr. Store time as i64 days plus f64 seconds.
- **GPU budget (estimate).** A 10-core M3 GPU peaks at about 3.6 TFLOPS FP32 with about 100 GB/s memory bandwidth. The Air has no fan, so plan for about 1–1.5 TFLOPS of useful sustained throughput. At 1920×1200 and 30 fps that is about 15k flops per pixel per frame for everything.

## 1. Real-time black-hole imaging on the GPU

### 1.1 Schwarzschild: cheapest exact method (recommended default)

The orbit equation is (du/dφ)² = 1/b² − u²(1 − rs·u), with u = 1/r. In second-order form it is u'' + u = (3/2)·rs·u².

Treat the path as a particle in flat 3D Cartesian space (x is the Schwarzschild r, θ, φ read as Euclidean coordinates) with this acceleration:

  d²x/dλ² = −(3/2) · rs · h² · x / |x|⁵,  where h = |x × ẋ| is constant.

This reproduces the null geodesic's spatial path exactly (from the Binet equation). The cost is about 20 flops per evaluation, and RK4 or leapfrog with step ∝ r works well.

The parameter λ is not affine, so take redshifts from the conserved quantities instead:
- b = L/E
- for a static camera: b = r_cam · sin δ / √(1 − rs/r_cam), where δ is the angle between the ray and the inward radial direction.

Termination:
- captured if r < rs(1 + ε)
- escaped if r > R_esc (≈ 1000M) and moving outward. The far region costs only ln(R_esc/r)/ε steps when h = ε·r.

**Exact lookup tables (LUTs).** Each ray stays in a plane, so for a camera at r_o every ray is set by one angle δ ∈ [0, π]:
- 1D LUT: δ → escape angle.
- 2D LUT: (δ, φ) → u = 1/r, for disk crossings. A thin disk crosses the ray plane along a line, so crossings sit at φ_line + nπ. For a volumetric disk, march φ through the table.

A table of about 2048×4096 f16 values is tiny. Rebuild it in a compute pass whenever r_o changes by more than a tolerance. That makes the cost O(1) per pixel. A moving camera is still exact: apply aberration to the local static-frame ray first (section 4).

**Closed forms**, if you want them instead of quadrature:
- Total deflection: Δφ(b) = 2∫₀^{u₀} du / √(1/b² − u² + rs·u³) − π. The exact elliptic-integral form is in Darwin (1959) and Iyer & Petters (2007).
- Weak field: α ≈ 2rs/b + (15π/16)(rs/b)².
- Strong-deflection limit (Bozza 2002): α ≈ −ln(b/b_c − 1) + ln[216(7 − 4√3)] − π, with b_c = 3√3·M ≈ 2.598·rs.
- Shadow angular radius from distance D: 3√3·GM/(c²D).

**Photon-ring physics.** Each extra half-orbit near b_c demagnifies the image by e^{−γ}, with Lyapunov exponent γ = π (Schwarzschild), about 1/23 (Johnson et al. 2020). Numerical errors grow by the same factor. Capping at 3–4 half-orbits loses nothing visible, and f32 resolves only about 5 anyway.

### 1.2 Kerr: choice of formulation

Boyer–Lindquist (BL) coordinates: Σ = r² + a²cos²θ, Δ = r² − 2Mr + a².

Use the super-Hamiltonian form, as in DNGR. James et al. (2015) say it is "well behaved and robust at turning points". With p_t = −1 (E = 1) and b = p_φ = L:

  2ΣH = Δ·p_r² + p_θ² − P²/Δ + (b − a·sin²θ)²/sin²θ = 0,  where P = r² + a² − ab

  Carter constant q = p_θ² + cos²θ·(b²/sin²θ − a²)

  R(r) = P² − Δ[(b − a)² + q],  Θ(θ) = q − cos²θ·(b²/sin²θ − a²)

The equations of motion are dx/dλ = ∂H/∂p and dp/dλ = −∂H/∂x. On shell:
- ṙ = Δ·p_r/Σ
- θ̇ = p_θ/Σ
- φ̇ = [a·P/Δ + b/sin²θ − a]/Σ
- ṗ_θ = (cos θ/Σ)·(b²/sin³θ − a²·sin θ)
- ṗ_r = −[Δ'·p_r² − 4r·P/Δ + P²·Δ'/Δ²]/(2Σ),  with Δ' = 2r − 2M

The weaknesses are a coordinate singularity at the horizon and pole artefacts.

**Cartesian Kerr–Schild (KS)** (GRay2, Chan et al. 2018). It has no pole or horizon singularity and is easy to pair with a Cartesian camera.

  g^{μν} = η^{μν} − f·l^μ·l^ν,  f = 2Mr³/(r⁴ + a²z²)

  l_μ = (1, (rx + ay)/(r² + a²), (ry − ax)/(r² + a²), z/r)

  r² = ½(ρ² − a²) + √(¼(ρ² − a²)² + a²z²),  ρ² = x² + y² + z²

  ∂r/∂x_i = (x_i·r² + a²·z·δ_iz) / (r·(2r² − ρ² + a²))

The Hamiltonian is H = ½·η^{μν}p_μp_ν − ½·f·(l·p)². That gives:
- ẋ^μ = η^{μν}p_ν − f·(l·p)·l^μ
- ṗ_i = ½·(∂_i f)·(l·p)² + f·(l·p)·(∂_i l^μ)·p_μ

The sign convention for a sets the spin direction, so test it against prograde frame dragging. Each step costs about 2–3× a BL step.

**Recommendation.** Use KS on the GPU. It is camera-friendly and needs no special cases, and it handles a camera that falls in. Use BL only to compute E, L and Q for the redshift factor g.

**Analytic Kerr.** Gralla & Lupsasca (PRD 101, 044032, 2020; use arXiv v3, which has corrections) give closed-form r(Mino time) and θ(Mino time) using Jacobi elliptic functions. The n-th equatorial crossing of a thin disk becomes O(1) per pixel through Carlson elliptic integrals; the AART and kgeo codes use this approach. This is the fastest correct Kerr disk renderer, but it is hard to implement. Treat it as a phase-2 option.

### 1.3 Integrators and step control

| Method | Evaluations per step | Order | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| RK4 | 4 | 4 | No error estimate. Fine with a heuristic step. |
| RKF45 (DNGR) | 6 | 5(4) | Adaptive. |
| Dormand–Prince 5(4) | 7, 6 effective (FSAL) | 5(4) | Adaptive. |

The adaptive methods usually take 2–5× fewer steps near the photon sphere and in the far field. They cost more GPU registers, but the M3's Dynamic Caching tolerates that well. In f32, relative tolerance below about 1e-6 means nothing; use rtol ≈ 1e-5.

Heuristic step size:
- h = ε·r, with ε ≈ 0.02–0.05 in the strong field
- additionally clamp h ≤ k·(r − r₊) near the horizon
- also clamp by the KS angular rate

Disk hits: detect the sign change of z (KS) or of θ − π/2 (BL), then refine with Hermite interpolation or one Newton step.

### 1.4 DNGR (James, von Tunzelmann, Franklin & Thorne 2015)

- Coordinates and frame: BL super-Hamiltonian with b and q, integrated with RKF. The camera is boosted relative to the FIDO/ZAMO frame.
- Ray bundles: Pineault–Roeder geodesic deviation is evolved per pixel to get each beam's elliptical footprint on the celestial sphere. This removed flicker but tripled the cost per step.
- Cost: 23M pixels per large-format film frame, 30 minutes to several hours per frame on 10 CPU cores.
- **Too expensive for real time.** Cheapest substitute: trace one ray per pixel, then take finite differences of the exit directions between neighbouring pixels to get the Jacobian J. Use J for the sky mip level and anisotropic footprint, and use μ = 1/|det J| to scale point-star brightness. Add temporal anti-aliasing.
- Movie choices: a uniform 4500 K thin disk, Doppler shifts omitted, and spin lowered to a = 0.6M for clarity. So the film's look is not physical. Ship the correct version by default.

### 1.5 Tiered pipeline (the budget makes this necessary)

**Cost estimate.** A KS RK4 step is about 600–1000 flops. At about 15k flops per pixel that buys only 15–25 steps, but a ray needs 60–150. **Full-screen, full-resolution Kerr tracing every frame is too expensive on an M3 Air.**

**Cheapest correct alternative:**
- **(a) Far field.** When the black hole is small on screen, deflect catalogue stars in the vertex shader with the point-lens solution. There are two images: θ± = (β ± √(β² + 4θ_E²))/2. Magnifications are μ± = (u² + 2)/(2u√(u² + 4)) ± ½. For a source at infinity, θ_E = √(2rs/D_l).
- **(b) Near field.**
  - Trace only inside a screen disk where deflection exceeds about 1 px.
  - Use the 1D/2D LUT for Schwarzschild and KS integration for Kerr.
  - Start rays at a sphere R₀ ≈ 50–100M, using straight lines plus the weak-field correction to reach it.
- **(c) Resolution.** Trace at half resolution and upsample with edge-aware filtering.
- **(d) Caching.** Cache a per-pixel G-buffer: exit direction, disk-hit (r, φ, n), g and μ. Animate the disk by advancing φ − Ω(r)·t without retracing. Retrace only when the camera moves relative to the black hole, and refine progressively while it moves.
- **Lensed scene geometry.** A planet seen through the lens can be ray-tested against geodesic segments (spheres only). Objects far behind can be fetched from an environment cube rendered at the camera. That carries a parallax error of about R_zone/distance; flag it.

### 1.6 Stars through the lens (the "nothing pixelated" requirement)

Do not lens a star texture. Find catalogue stars near each pixel's exit direction with an angular hash (cube-face grid). Evaluate a PSF scaled by μ and by the Doppler factor. Stars stay sharp at any magnification.

### 1.7 Wormhole jumps (visual)

Use the Ellis / Morris–Thorne metric:

  ds² = −dt² + dℓ² + r(ℓ)²·dΩ²,  r = √(ρ² + ℓ²) in the simplest case

Ray equations (James et al. 2015, AJP 83, 486):
- dℓ/dt = p_ℓ
- dθ/dt = p_θ/r²
- dφ/dt = b/(r²·sin²θ)
- dp_ℓ/dt = B²·(dr/dℓ)/r³
- dp_θ/dt = b²·cos θ/(r²·sin³θ)
- where B² = p_θ² + b²/sin²θ

The geometry is spherically symmetric, so the same 1D LUT trick applies. Rays with b < ρ sample the destination sky, which can be a live cube map rendered at the destination.

Label this as speculative: a traversable wormhole needs exotic matter that violates the null energy condition (Morris & Thorne 1988).

### 1.8 Neutron stars (cheap and good)

Beloborodov (2002) bending approximation: 1 − cos α = (1 − cos ψ)(1 − rs/R).

## 2. Accretion disks

### 2.1 Circular equatorial Kerr orbits (Bardeen, Press & Teukolsky 1972; upper sign = prograde)

- Ω = ±M^{1/2} / (r^{3/2} ± a·M^{1/2})
- u^t = (r^{3/2} ± a·M^{1/2}) / [r^{3/4}·√(r^{3/2} − 3M·r^{1/2} ± 2a·M^{1/2})]
- E and L: the standard BPT expressions
- ISCO: Z₁ = 1 + (1 − a²)^{1/3}·[(1 + a)^{1/3} + (1 − a)^{1/3}], Z₂ = √(3a² + Z₁²), r_ms = M·{3 + Z₂ ∓ √[(3 − Z₁)(3 + Z₁ + 2Z₂)]}
- Equatorial photon orbit: r_ph = 2M·{1 + cos[(2/3)·arccos(∓a)]}
- Horizon: r₊ = M + √(M² − a²)
- Efficiency η = 1 − E_isco: 0.057 for a = 0, 0.32 for a = 0.998

### 2.2 Novikov–Thorne / Page–Thorne flux (zero torque at the ISCO)

  F(r) = −(Ṁ / (4π√−g)) · Ω_{,r} / (E − ΩL)² · ∫_{r_ms}^{r} (E − ΩL)·L_{,r} dr,  with √−g = r on the equator

Compute this once on the CPU as a 1D LUT. Then T_eff(r) = (F/σ)^{1/4}.

Newtonian check (Shakura–Sunyaev): σT⁴ = (3GMṀ / (8πr³))·(1 − √(r_in/r)), peaking at r = (49/36)·r_in.

My worked numbers:
- 10 M☉ at the Eddington limit: T_max ≈ 9×10⁶ K (about 0.8 keV).
- T scales as M^{−1/4}·(Ṁ/Ṁ_Edd)^{1/4}, so about 1.5×10⁵ K at 10⁸ M☉.

Real disks render blue-white in the visible band (the Rayleigh–Jeans colour limit). They are not orange.

Optional refinements:
- Colour correction: I_ν = B_ν(f_col·T)/f_col⁴, with f_col ≈ 1.7 (Shimura & Takahara 1995).
- Limb darkening: I ∝ 1 + 2.06μ (Chandrasekhar electron-scattering atmosphere), where μ is the emission cosine in the fluid frame.
- Plunging region inside the ISCO: the flow keeps E_isco and L_isco and shows faint emission (Cunningham 1975; the "inner shadow" of Chael et al. 2021).

### 2.3 Redshift factor

General form: g = (p·u_cam)/(p·u_em). For a static observer at infinity and an emitter on a circular orbit:

  g = 1 / [u^t·(1 − Ω·λ)],  λ = L_z/E of the photon

- Schwarzschild: g = √(1 − 3M/r) / (1 − Ωλ).
- With the Binet tracer, λ = b·(ĥ·ẑ), where ĥ is the unit normal of the ray plane.
- For a static camera at r_c, divide by √(1 − rs/r_c).
- This one formula covers the Doppler, transverse and gravitational shifts.

### 2.4 Intensity

I_ν/ν³ is invariant, so:
- spectral: I_obs,ν(ν) = g³·I_em,ν(ν/g)
- bolometric: I_obs = g⁴·I_em

For blackbody emitters, simply shade with Planck at T_obs = g·T. That automatically gives both the colour shift and the g³/g⁴ beaming.

### 2.5 Blackbody → CIE XYZ → sRGB

Planck: B_λ(T) = (2hc²/λ⁵) / (e^{hc/(λkT)} − 1).

### 2.6 Thin vs volumetric disks

- A Novikov–Thorne disk has H/r ≈ 0.01–0.05, so it appears razor-thin. A surface emitter is the correct model for it.
- Puffy volumetric looks are physical only for thick flows:
  - slim, super-Eddington disks
  - radiatively inefficient accretion flows (RIAFs), such as Sgr A* and M87*, which are optically thin and synchrotron-dominated
- For those, integrate the covariant transfer equation along the geodesic: d(I/ν³)/dλ = j/ν² − (να)(I/ν³). Use parametric RIAF profiles, about 32–64 samples per crossing, and a cached 3D noise texture.
- **Too expensive:** general-relativistic MHD (GRMHD) snapshots and polarized transfer. Gigabytes of data and slow; skip both.

### 2.7 Turbulence with differential rotation

Real turbulence comes from the magnetorotational instability (MRI; Balbus & Hawley 1991). It forms trailing filaments stretched along the orbit.

Model it with noise in the co-rotating frame, φ' = φ − Ω(r)·t. Plain advection winds the pattern up without limit, so use advected-texture blending (Neyret 2003): two copies with phases t mod T and (t + T/2) mod T, cross-faded.

### 2.8 Jets

- Power source: Blandford–Znajek (1977).
- Geometry: parabolic funnel, z ∝ R^{~1.6} for M87 (Asada & Nakamura 2012).
- Bulk Lorentz factor Γ ≈ 2–10. Doppler factor δ = 1/[Γ(1 − β·cos θ)].
- Specific intensity scales as δ^{3+α} for a blob and δ^{2+α} for a steady jet.
- Emission is optically thin synchrotron.

## 3. Hawking radiation

Core formulas:
- **Temperature:** T_H = ħc³/(8πGMk_B) ≈ 6.17×10⁻⁸ K × (M☉/M). In energy units, kT ≈ 1.06 GeV × (10¹³ g / M).
- **Photon-only blackbody power:** P = ħc⁶/(15360·π·G²·M²) ≈ 3.56×10³² W × (kg/M)², which is 9×10⁻²⁹ W for 1 M☉.
- **Naive lifetime:** t = 5120·π·G²·M³/(ħc⁴) ≈ 8.4×10⁻¹⁷ s × (M/kg)³, about 2×10⁶⁷ yr × (M/M☉)³.

With greybody factors and all particle species included:
- Mass-loss rate: dM/dt = −5.34×10²⁵·f(M)/M² g/s (MacGibbon 1991), with f = 1 when only massless species are emitted.
- Lifetime: τ ≈ 407·(f/15.35)⁻¹·(M/10¹⁰ g)³ s. For massless-only emission that gives about 2×10⁶⁶ yr × (M/M☉)³ (Page 1976).
- Primordial black holes born at M* ≈ 5×10¹⁴ g (5×10¹¹ kg) are evaporating now (Carr et al. 2010).
- Photon emission peaks near 5.7·kT_H.

Where it is visible:
- **Equal to the CMB** at M ≈ 4.5×10²² kg (about 0.6 Moon masses). Anything heavier absorbs more than it emits and grows. For 10 M☉, geometric CMB absorption (σ = 27πM²) gives about 230 W of input, against 9×10⁻³¹ W of Hawking output.
- **A visible-light Hawking glow** (about 6000 K) needs M ≈ 2×10¹⁹ kg. Then rs ≈ 30 nm and P ≈ 1 µW. **No mass is both visible-coloured and bright.**
- **Luminous only below about 10¹¹–10¹² kg.** Those are primordial micro black holes emitting gamma rays and particles at roughly 10¹¹–10¹² W. The final second releases about 10²³ J.

Showing it honestly:
- For stellar and supermassive black holes, show a HUD readout of T_H, P_H, τ_evap and the net dM/dt from CMB and accretion compared with Hawking loss. Render nothing.
- Procedural primordial black holes are point gamma sources. Integrate the MacGibbon dM/dt with an f(M) table and end with the final flash.
- Any exaggerated "Hawking view" must be an explicitly labelled false-colour instrument mode.
- **Too expensive:** exact greybody spectra. Cheapest alternative: a blackbody times a tabulated f(M) scale factor.

**Sources:** Hawking 1975, CMP 43, 199 · Page 1976, PRD 13, 198 · MacGibbon 1991, PRD 44, 376 · [Carr et al. 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.5297) · [Carr et al. 2020 review](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.12778).

## 4. Relativistic moving-observer effects (optional realism mode)

Let s' be the apparent direction to a source in the camera frame and s the direction in the world frame. With β the camera velocity in the world frame:

  s = [s' + (γ − 1)(s'·β̂)·β̂ − γ·β] / [γ·(1 − β·s')]

  D = ν_obs/ν_world = 1/[γ·(1 − β·s')] = γ·(1 + β·s)

In the angle form: cos θ' = (cos θ + β)/(1 + β·cos θ).

How to apply it:
- Do it per pixel in ray generation, before any tracing. It is O(1) and exact for scenes at rest in the world frame.
- **Extended sources** (sky, disk, surfaces): I'_ν'(ν') = D³·I_ν(ν'/D); bolometric I' = D⁴·I.
- **Point sources** (stars): bolometric flux F' = D²·F, so Δm = −5·log₁₀D, and T' = D·T. I checked this against the energy-density transform of a collimated beam.
- **Moving emitters** with a static observer: δ⁴ bolometric; this matters only for jets.
- **General emitter and observer:** ν_o/ν_e = γ_o(1 − β_o·k̂) / [γ_e(1 − β_e·k̂)], with k̂ the propagation direction.
- **Star temperatures from catalogue colour:** T_eff = 4600·[1/(0.92(B−V) + 1.7) + 1/(0.92(B−V) + 0.62)] (Ballesteros 2012).
- **RGB textures and reflected light** (the Milky Way band, planets): Doppler-shifting RGB is ill-defined. Use Jakob & Hanika (2019) 3-coefficient spectral upsampling from a 3D LUT, shift the spectrum, and re-integrate over about 8–16 wavelength samples.
- **CMB:** T' = 2.725·D K. It reaches the visible only when D ≈ 1000.
- **Finite light-travel time for moving objects:** solve the retarded position per object on the CPU with one Newton iteration.

**Ship kinematics.** Store proper velocity w = γv, which is unbounded. Update with dw/dt = (F'_∥ + F'_⊥/γ)/m and dτ = dt/γ, then v = w/√(1 + w²/c²). Constant proper acceleration a gives:
- v = c·tanh(aτ/c)
- t = (c/a)·sinh(aτ/c)
- x = (c²/a)·(cosh(aτ/c) − 1)

"Faster than light" must be a separate warp or jump mode with no special-relativity effects, and labelled as such.

**Sources:** Weiskopf, Kraus & Ruder 1999, ACM TOG 18(3), 278 (searchlight and Doppler effects) · McKinley & Doherty 1979, AJP 47, 309 · Jakob & Hanika 2019, CGF 38(2) · Ballesteros 2012, EPL 97, 34008.

## 5. Gravity on the player's ship

**Patched conics** (one dominant body at a time) are approximate, not "real". Useful formulas:
- Sphere of influence: r_SOI = a·(m/M)^{2/5}. For Earth that is about 9.25×10⁵ km.
- Hill sphere: r_H = a(1 − e)·(m/3M)^{1/3}, about 1.5×10⁶ km for Earth.

**Recommended: restricted n-body.**
- Bodies stay on analytic rails (section 6). Their positions at any time t are evaluated directly, so time warp costs nothing for them.
- The ship is a test particle feeling the summed gravity of all bodies above a threshold, using f64 on the CPU.
- Integrator: adaptive Dormand–Prince, or IAS15 for close encounters. Use Encke's method (perturbations relative to a reference Kepler orbit) far from bodies.
- When coasting deep inside one sphere of influence, propagate the Kepler orbit analytically for unbounded warp. Fall back to numerical integration when the perturbation estimate grows.
- Predict trajectories for the HUD on a background CPU thread.
- Add the 1PN term from the Sun: a_GR = (GM/(c²r³))·[(4GM/r − v²)·r + 4(r·v)·v]. It gives Mercury's 43″ per century.

**Near black holes**, inside about 100 rs: integrate the true timelike geodesic with thrust, du^μ/dτ + Γ^μ_αβ·u^α·u^β = a^μ (with a·u = 0), in f64 KS coordinates. One particle is trivially cheap.
- Pseudo-Newtonian fallback: Paczyński–Wiita potential Φ = −GM/(r − rs). It reproduces the ISCO at 6M.
- HUD readouts:
  - time dilation: dτ/dt = √(1 − rs/r) when static, √(1 − 3M/r) on a circular orbit
  - tidal stretch: Δa ≈ 2GM·ℓ/r³
  - hover thrust: GM/[r²·√(1 − rs/r)], which diverges at the horizon

**Keeping free flight usable without faking physics.** Gravity always acts; the assists are engine-driven:
- **Inertial:** no assist.
- **Station-keep:** the engine cancels the summed gravity and shows the thrust it needs.
- **Orbit-hold / circularize:** autopilot.
- **Cruise / jump:** sci-fi modes in which gravity is deliberately ignored, clearly labelled.

**Time-warp limit for numerical flight:** step < T_orbit/50 for the nearest relevant body. Show the gravity vector and its magnitude.

**Sources:** Paczyński & Wiita 1980, A&A 88, 23 · Rein & Spiegel 2015, MNRAS 446, 1424 (IAS15) · Battin, *Astronautics* (Encke's method, sphere of influence) · Principia (an open-source mod for a commercial space game), for the restricted n-body approach only; I looked at none of its code.

## 6. Orbital mechanics for real bodies

**JPL "Keplerian Elements for Approximate Positions of the Major Planets"** (Standish), [ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/planets/approx_pos.html). Steps:

1. T = (JD_TDB − 2451545.0)/36525.
2. Each element is x = x₀ + ẋ·T, for a, e, I, L, ϖ, Ω.
3. ω = ϖ − Ω.
4. M = L − ϖ + bT² + c·cos(fT) + s·sin(fT). The b, c, s, f terms apply to Jupiter–Neptune and are required with Table 2a.
5. Solve E − e*·sin E = M (e* = 57.29578·e, in degrees):
   - E₀ = M + e*·sin M
   - ΔE = [M − (E − e*·sin E)] / (1 − e·cos E)
   - stop at tolerance 1e-6 degrees
6. x' = a(cos E − e), y' = a·√(1 − e²)·sin E.
7. Rotate by (ω, I, Ω) into the J2000 ecliptic frame.
8. Rotate into the equatorial frame (ICRF) with ε = 23.43928°.

Validity:
- Table 1: 1800–2050.
- Tables 2a and 2b: 3000 BC – 3000 AD.

Nominal errors for 1800–2050:

| Body | Longitude error | Distance error |
|---|---|---|
| Mercury | 15″ | 1000 km |
| Earth–Moon barycentre | 20″ | 6000 km |
| Mars | 40″ | 25,000 km |
| Jupiter | 400″ | 600,000 km |
| Saturn | 600″ | 1.5×10⁶ km |
| Uranus | 50″ | 10⁶ km |
| Neptune | 10″ | 2×10⁵ km |

This is visually perfect. It covers the Earth–Moon barycentre only; the Moon needs its own theory.

**Moons:**
- Earth's Moon: Meeus ch. 47 (truncated ELP-2000/82, about 10″).
- Other moons: JPL SSD mean elements with apsidal and nodal precession about the Laplace plane, [ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem/). JPL notes these are "not intended for ephemeris computation", but they are fine visually.
- Galilean moons: Meeus ch. 44.

**Rotation and axial tilt:** IAU WGCCRE 2015 report (Archinal et al. 2018, CMDA 130:22, with a 2019 erratum). It is still the latest main report.
- Inputs: pole α₀(T), δ₀(T) and prime meridian W = W₀ + Ẇ·d, with d in days from J2000 TDB.
- Body-fixed → ICRF: R = R₃(−(90° + α₀))·R₁(−(90° − δ₀))·R₃(−W).
- Earth: α₀ = 0.00 − 0.641T, δ₀ = 90.00 − 0.557T, W = 190.147 + 360.9856235·d.
- Sun: α₀ = 286.13, δ₀ = 63.87, W = 84.176 + 14.1844·d.
- The Moon and Mars need periodic terms.
- Axial tilt follows from the pole vector against the orbit normal.

**Beyond ±3000 yr of warp.** Switch to secular (Laplace–Lagrange) element evolution, which is analytic at any time. The inner Solar System is chaotic with a Lyapunov time of about 5 Myr (Laskar 1989), so no deterministic ephemeris exists past about 50 Myr. Secular evolution is the honest limit.

**Sources:** Standish & Williams 1992 (via the JPL page) · [WGCCRE 2015 report](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/report-iau-working-group-cartographic-coordinates-and-rotational-elements-2015) · Meeus, *Astronomical Algorithms*, 2nd ed. · Park et al. 2021, AJ 161, 105 (DE440/441) · Laskar 1989, Nature 338, 237.

## 7. N-body integrators

**Leapfrog (kick–drift–kick):**
- v½ = v + (h/2)·a(x)
- x' = x + h·v½
- v' = v½ + (h/2)·a(x')

It is symplectic and time-reversible with bounded energy error, but only with a fixed or time-symmetric step.

**Wisdom–Holman:** split H = H_Kepler + H_interaction. Drift with a universal-variable Kepler solver (Gauss f and g functions; Wisdom & Hernandez 2015) and kick with the interaction term.
- WHFast adds symplectic correctors (Rein & Tamayo 2015).
- Step about 1/20 of the shortest orbital period. Energy error is about 1e-9.
- Best for procedural planetary systems: 8 planets cost microseconds per step.
- Use IAS15 or MERCURY-style hybrids for close encounters.
- Stable procedural spacing: more than about 10 mutual Hill radii (Chambers et al. 1996). Such systems can then sit on Kepler rails with analytic secular precession.

**Star clusters:**
- Plummer initial conditions via Aarseth, Hénon & Wielen (1974).
- Direct GPU summation with tiling (GPU Gems 3, ch. 31) up to about 3×10⁴ stars per frame. Use f32 with softening and leapfrog, or 4th-order Hermite with block timesteps for collisional realism (Makino & Aarseth 1992).
- **Cheapest correct alternative for large clusters and galaxies:** test particles in an analytic smooth potential, O(N). This is exact while the elapsed time is much shorter than the relaxation time t_relax ≈ (0.1N/ln N)·t_cross, i.e. Gyr for globular clusters.

**Galactic potential:**
- MWPotential2014 (Bovy 2015): power-law bulge with cutoff, Miyamoto–Nagai disk Φ = −GM/√(R² + (a + √(z² + b²))²), and NFW halo Φ = −4πGρ₀·r_s³·ln(1 + r/r_s)/r.
- Or the McMillan (2017) potential.
- Leapfrog with dt ≈ 0.1–1 Myr.

**Real stars:** propagate (α, δ, ϖ, µ, v_r) as linear 3D motion for up to about 10⁵ yr, then switch to integration in the potential.

**Hypervelocity stars** (Hills mechanism; Hills 1988):
- Ejection speed v_ej ≈ 1760 km/s × (a_bin/0.1 AU)^{−1/2} × (m_bin/2 M☉)^{1/3} × (M_BH/3.5×10⁶ M☉)^{1/6} (Bromley et al. 2006).
- Real example: S5-HVS1 at 1755 km/s (Koposov et al. 2020).
- Galactic escape speed near the Sun is about 530 km/s.
- Supernova runaways move at about 30–200 km/s.
- Use real S-star orbits such as S2 (P = 16.05 yr, e = 0.886) to show Schwarzschild precession.

**Barnes–Hut:**
- Octree of node mass and centre of mass (optionally quadrupoles). Accept a node if s/d < θ, with θ ≈ 0.5–0.7 for about 0.1–1% force error.
- CPU: rayon with Morton ordering. My estimate is about 10⁵ particles interactively on an 8-core M3. 10⁶ particles take about 0.5–1 s per step, which is **too slow on the CPU**.
- GPU: Morton-sorted linear octree/BVH built in parallel (Karras 2012), per Burtscher & Pingali (2011).
- Caveat: core WGSL has only 32-bit integer atomics, so use fixed-point or reduction passes for accumulating node mass.
- Alternative: FMM (Dehnen 2002).
- Full-galaxy self-gravity of 10¹¹ stars is impossible; use the potential plus test particles.

**Sources:** Wisdom & Holman 1991, AJ 102, 1528 · Rein & Tamayo 2015, MNRAS 452, 376 · Wisdom & Hernandez 2015, MNRAS 453, 3015 · Barnes & Hut 1986, Nature 324, 446 · Burtscher & Pingali 2011, GPU Computing Gems ch. 6 · Karras 2012, HPG · Nyland, Harris & Prins 2007, GPU Gems 3 ch. 31 · [Bovy 2015 (galpy)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.3451) · McMillan 2017, MNRAS 465, 76 · Hills 1988, Nature 331, 687 · Bromley et al. 2006, ApJ 653, 1194 · Koposov et al. 2020, MNRAS 491, 2465 · Chambers et al. 1996, Icarus 119, 261.

## Summary: correct but too expensive on M3, and the cheapest correct replacement

| Too expensive | Cheapest physically correct alternative |
|---|---|
| DNGR per-pixel ray bundles (hours per frame) | One ray per pixel plus a screen-space Jacobian for footprint and magnification, plus temporal anti-aliasing. |
| Full-screen, full-resolution Kerr RK every frame | Weak-field star lensing in the vertex shader; trace only the lensing region at half resolution; cache the G-buffer and rotate the disk analytically; Gralla–Lupsasca closed form in phase 2. |
| Schwarzschild via a general Hamiltonian | Binet-form Cartesian ODE, or the exact 1D/2D LUT keyed by δ (O(1) per pixel). |
| GRMHD volumes / polarized transfer | Novikov–Thorne thin disk, or a parametric RIAF with advected noise. |
| Spectral rendering everywhere | Blackbody at T_obs = gT (or D·T) through an XYZ LUT; Jakob–Hanika upsampling only for RGB textures. |
| Exact greybody Hawking spectra | Blackbody times an f(M) table, HUD only. |
| Galaxy-scale n-body | Analytic potential plus test particles; direct GPU n-body only for clusters up to about 3×10⁴ stars. |
| DE441 ephemeris (3.1 GB) | Standish elements (1 KB); optional DE440s (32 MB). |
| GPU f64 | Not available on Metal at all: CPU f64 and i128 world coordinates, camera-relative f32 on the GPU. |

Two figures above are my own estimates and not from a source: the M3 throughput and per-pixel flop budgets, and the Barnes–Hut CPU timings. Treat them as order-of-magnitude until they are measured on the machine.
